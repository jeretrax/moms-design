# Benefit and Time Model

Status: source-backed requirements. Sources: H2, H3, H4.

Every material recommendation explains the action, why it matters, expected benefit, approximate useful measurement window, cost, effort, dependencies, confidence/evidence, and measurement method.

| Class | Examples | Customer meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate / Activation | Paid visibility after activation; correcting broken basics or listing errors | An activity or correction may take effect quickly; business results are unproven |
| Near-Term Measurement | Paid optimization, landing-page tests, conversion work, local listing changes, review activity | Gather enough relevant observations to evaluate a change |
| Longer-Term Measurement | Organic ranking, authority, content portfolio, competitive visibility | Effects may accumulate and require longer observation |

These are categories, not fixed day counts or service-level guarantees. Each useful window depends on activation, traffic, source availability, dependencies, and evidence. When a numerical estimate is unsupported, retain the category and explain what is needed to estimate the window.

## Proposed measurement record

Link customer, goal, work item, hypothesis version, expected direction/range if supportable, metric/source, baseline value and date window, activation date, earliest useful review condition, assumptions, confidence, and later observations. Preserve original forecasts and distinguish revised forecasts. Final attribution and statistical rules are D-009.

Compare compatible metrics, source definitions, geographies, currencies, and time windows. Explain incomplete tracking, low volume, seasonality, concurrent changes, or other confounders. Observed improvement after work is not automatically proof of causation.

A later result may be improved, unchanged, worsened, or inconclusive as a proposed reporting vocabulary. Do not guarantee organic ranking/date outcomes or conflate impressions, clicks, conversions, qualified leads, and revenue.
